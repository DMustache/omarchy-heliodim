heliodim

Laptop screen brightness that follows the sun, for Omarchy. Bright at noon, dim after dusk, and when you press the brightness keys, your choice stays.

Install Source on GitHub
  • one static Zig binary, ~230 KB
  • under 1 MB of memory, one thread
  • no root, no network

The sun in your bar

The bar icon shows how high the sun stands where you are: stars at night, a sliver at twilight, a half disc at sunrise, a rayed disc high in the sky. Mornings put the sun on the left, evenings on the right. It draws in the bar's text color, so it fits every Omarchy theme.

Demo place: Moscow, 55.75° N 37.62° E, local time UTC+3. The slider drives the bar, the states and the panel below.

How it works

The sun

Its position comes from the same subsolar-point formula Omarchy's Elsewhen clock draws its daylight strip with, so both agree on sunrise. Clear-sky light comes from the Haurwitz model, and the brightness curve is min + (max − min) · √(GHI / 1000): 10° of sun gives about 37% of the range, 30° about 70%.

A schedule, not polling

At start heliodim reads max_brightness and turns one step (1% by default) into raw units, for example 655 of 65535 on amdgpu. After each change it finds the next minute when the target is a whole step away and sleeps on a wall-clock timer until then. A night is a single sleep. Suspend and clock changes wake it on time.

Your hand wins

The kernel signals every brightness change, so the brightness keys are seen at once. After 2 quiet seconds heliodim keeps the difference from the curve as an offset. The curve keeps moving with the sun, and your correction moves with it. The offset survives restarts.

Ready to learn

Each hand change is logged with the sun's state at that moment, to learn cloudy-day corrections later. One line per change in ~/.local/state/heliodim/events.jsonl:

{"t":1794414004,"pct":88.00,
 "curve":95.00,"offset":-7.00,
 "elev":-23.75,"az":273.5,"clear":0}

Fits Omarchy

Brightness goes through brightnessctl and logind, the same path as Omarchy's brightness keys. Night light (hyprsunset) changes the color, heliodim changes the brightness. Big gaps after resume fade in over at most 10 steps, and a switched-off screen is left alone.

A left click on the bar icon opens this panel.

Sky plots today's sun from midnight to midnight. The part above the horizon is the day, the dot is now, and the ticks mark sunrise, noon and sunset.

Brightness is the value on screen. The thin accent mark on the meter is where the sun curve alone would put it, and the gap between them is your offset.

Pause, or a middle click on the icon, leaves brightness to you until the next sunrise or sunset. Forget offset returns to the plain curve. From a terminal, use the plugin's bin/heliodim launcher with pause or reset.

The offset and its note are demo values.

Where you are

heliodim takes the first of these it finds. Nothing is sent over the network.

  1. lat and lon in ~/.config/heliodim/config
  2. the “here” city of the Elsewhen clock, while the system time zone is still that city's zone
  3. Omarchy's weather location, set with omarchy-weather-location --set NAME LAT,LON
  4. your time zone's reference city, from /etc/localtime and tzdata's zone1970.tab

Install

One command adds the bar plugin and its ready-to-run Linux binary. The plugin selects x86_64 or aarch64 automatically and runs the daemon. No Zig needed. Omarchy includes brightnessctl. Built-in laptop screens only for now.

omarchy plugin add https://github.com/DMustache/omarchy-heliodim --enable

Config

# ~/.config/heliodim/config, every key optional
lat = 55.75
lon = 37.62
min = 10      # % at night
max = 100     # % at the top of the curve
step = 1      # % per change
device = amdgpu_bl1

Check it

$ heliodim status
